<h2>Information revealed by combining observations</h2>
<p>Suppose we compare two language models using human ratings. Each rating combines model quality with the rater's personal offset. Asking one person to rate both models removes that offset, exposing the quality difference. This is the simplest example of <strong>target emergence</strong>.</p>
<p>We study acquisition when observations share nuisance effects and develop entry-fee (EF) scoring, which evaluates an informative bundle before opening a new group. On PRISM preference contrasts, the reported approach reduced mean identification cost from <strong>20.43 ratings to 2</strong>.</p>
<p>The same principle appears in blocked experiments, repeated measures, and multi-sensor designs: sometimes the relevant unit of information is a carefully chosen set of observations.</p>
